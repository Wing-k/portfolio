Mobile App Sustainability 48-hour Sprint

Raccoon

A mobile app aimed to encourage healthy eco-conscious habits with a dashboard, scavenger map, trash sorting game, and trash infographics — bridging the knowledge gap between people and their contribution towards recycling and donations.

Problem

How can we take part in being more eco-conscious and inspire others to also take action?

Goals

  • (1) Increasing accessibility: Provide easily accessible information and resources about trash sorting (e.g. what falls into each trash category) for everyone.
  • (2) Intrinsic Motivation: Intrinsically motivate people to recycle and donate with a playful scavenger hunt & dashboard.
  • (3) Easy Navigation: Encourage an eco-conscious lifestyle with an intuitive UI map that allows users to find where they can donate/recycle easily.

Impacts & Outcomes

Research
  • Conducted a competitive analysis of multiple recycling information sources and uncovered key knowledge gaps, informing prioritization of features like the scavenger map and trash sorting game.
Design
  • Designed a playful scavenger hunt experience and interactive infographics, boosting potential user engagement by pairing education with motivation, and providing clear, map-driven access to recycling/donation locations.
Deliverable
  • Produced a polished interactive prototype within a 48-hour design sprint in a team of 3 UX designers, earning the Best Sustainability Hack Award out of 56 projects for real-world impact and focus on environmental behavior change.
Solution — Raccoon

Raccoon aimed to encourage healthy eco-conscious habits with the following features:

01

Dashboard

Statistics and graphs showing user impact on the environment.

02

Scavenger Map

Map with scavenger hunt stars that award and motivate people to visit locations and recycle/donate.

03

Search

Simple interface allowing users to easily find the nearest location and understand what category their trash belongs to.

04

Trash Sorting Game

Tests user knowledge on categorizing trash while teaching them as they play.

05

Trash Bin Infographics

Users can click into different bins and learn about the types within one waste category and the items under it.

Research — Competitive Analysis

Even though multiple platforms provide recycling resources — SF Recycles, Recology, SF Environment, RE:Source, CalRecycle — they often do not indicate a recycling/donation location clearly on a map nor motivate people to take action.

  • New York Post: "32% of Americans are unable to correctly identify the universal recycling symbol signifying a product can be recycled."
  • Clean Air Task Force: "The waste sector is responsible for about 20% of human-driven methane emissions globally."
  • CalRecycle: In 2020, California produced 77.4 million tons of trash. Of that, the state landfilled 40 million tons and recycled just 42%.

Takeaways

This project provided a unique, end-to-end experience in a 48-hour design sprint. It taught me how to combine technical expertise, collaborative teamwork, and purpose-driven design to tackle real-world challenges.

  • Adapting Under Pressure: The tight deadline required us to be highly adaptable and focused. I learned how to prioritize research, make rapid design decisions, and prototype efficiently to deliver a cohesive final product.
  • Technical Skill Expansion: I significantly expanded my Figma skills, specifically in mastering animations and components to create a smooth and interactive experience for our trash-sorting game.
  • Purposeful Design: I gained a deeper appreciation for using design as a tool for social good. This project reinforced the importance of creating user-centered solutions that not only solve a problem but also drive awareness and impact.
