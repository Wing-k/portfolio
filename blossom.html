Social Computing UX Design April 2023 – September 2024

Blossom

A platform developed from research related to designing co-created community events that foster interactions in the Tech4Good lab, a Social Computing Lab, at the University of California Santa Cruz.

Role
UI/UX Designer & Researcher
Duration
1 year 6 months
Team
8 UI/UX Designers & Researchers
Tasks
User Research UX Design Interactive Prototype Prototype Studies

Problem

  • (1) How might we support goals such as icebreaking, relationship building, information exchange, and collaborative action?
  • (2) How might we engage participants themselves in shaping the activity dynamics to better fit their collective goals?

Goals

  • (1) Collocated community space: Establish an environment where participants feel comfortable engaging with one another, seeking advice, and sharing knowledge.
  • (2) Inclusive design approach: Allow participants to shape the dynamics of the activity, ensuring it aligns with their personal or collective goals.

Impacts & Outcomes

Research
  • Conducted literature review and thematic analysis to support research and design exploration.
  • Contributed to 10+ in-depth interviews and 40+ protostudy participants, uncovering critical clarity and engagement issues that limited user participation.
  • Synthesized qualitative and quantitative insights into actionable design recommendations that guided subsequent iterations.
Design
  • Designed digital 5×5 bingo cards that supported both individual and group use.
Usability & Iteration
  • Conducted iterative prototype testing and analysis, refining prompt wording and interaction flow based on direct user feedback.
Process

Research — Literature Review & Ideation

To initiate the project, I collaborated with my team on a literature review phase, where I scoped papers of relevant themes on relationship building and collocated community. I focused on analyzing existing research to identify successes and gaps in current solutions for fostering event interaction. By synthesizing our findings and drawing out common themes, we were able to see that there was a need for a more personal, face-to-face interactive element. This led us to explore different game concepts and propose a "Human Bingo" game, which best aligned with our primary objective of improving student interaction and fostering a more connected classroom environment.

Initial Design

I translated our research insights into a prototype design by reimagining the classic Bingo game. My team and I developed the concept for a digital 5×5 Bingo card, with one view for students on their mobile devices and another for a public projector. This design was specifically created to encourage both individual and group participation.

To ensure the game's content was relevant and engaging, we created the Bingo card prompts using answers from a pre-event student survey. This process of co-creation was central to our design, making sure the game was directly influenced by the students' own experiences and interests.

User Testing & Data Synthesis

Using the initial prototype, I helped conduct 3 protostudies with over 40 participants to gather feedback. I personally conducted over 10 user interviews and analyzed the resulting survey data to pinpoint key user pain points.

Our research revealed a lack of clarity in the prompt questions — leading to a key design decision: categorizing the prompts to make them easier for users to understand.

New Design

Based on insights from our user studies, we refined the bingo prompts into 4 prompt categories to improve clarity and create a more intentional experience. This revision directly addresses the user confusion we identified and helps guide participants toward specific goals:

01

Connections

Build relationships by helping people find new friends with shared interests.

02

Support

Foster connections beyond the game by encouraging the exchange of advice and tips.

03

Have Fun

Establish a relaxing, stress-free environment that makes the activity more enjoyable.

04

Ideate

Deepen connections by encouraging discussion of needs, ideas, and new perspectives.

Research Paper & Future Work

With the updated prototype, I helped conduct user studies in real classroom settings to test the design's usability and effectiveness. Afterwards, I conducted post-event interviews and thematic analysis to gather deeper insights on user pain points and satisfaction. The insights were used to refine the bingo cards for both mobile and projector views, optimizing the final user experience. I also contributed to the documentation of these findings for the team's ongoing research paper.

Takeaways

This project was my first full-cycle UX experience, and it taught me how to navigate the complexities of a real-world product.

  • The Power of Direction: Initially, the project lacked a clear focus, which slowed our progress. I learned the critical importance of a well-defined problem statement and a focused research goal to drive an efficient and effective design process.
  • Balancing Flexibility and Focus: While it's crucial to have a clear direction, this project taught me that the path to the final solution is rarely linear. I learned to balance a commitment to our core objective with the flexibility needed to adapt to user feedback and new insights.
  • The Full Picture: By taking the project from initial research and ideation all the way to a final, tested prototype, I gained a comprehensive understanding of the UX workflow and how research and design work together to create a validated solution.
